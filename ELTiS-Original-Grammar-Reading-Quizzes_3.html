ELTiS Practice Test

Grammar
Directions: In this part of the test, you will read some sentences and choose the best answer to complete each sentence.
1. The tourists waiting in front of the historic library ________ quiet.
正解: B. are
【解説】主語は複数名詞の `The tourists`（修飾語句：waiting in front of...）のため、複数に対応する be動詞 are が入ります。
2. ________ you enjoy listening to classical music after studying?
正解: C. Do
【解説】一般動詞 `enjoy` の二人称（you）に対する現在形の疑問文を作るため、Do が正解です。
3. Complicated math explanations often make the students ________.
正解: B. confused
【解説】`make + 目的語 + 形容詞`（Oを〜な状態にする）。人間（the students）が混乱させられる感情・状態を表すため、過去分詞形容詞の confused（戸惑って、困惑して）が正解です。
4. ________ this poem written by a famous American author in the 1950s?
正解: A. Was
【解説】過去の受動態（was/were + 過去分詞）の疑問文。「この詩は書かれましたか」という意味を作るため、単数主語 this poem に対応する Was が正解です。
5. The principal decided ________ the students a longer lunch break on Friday.
正解: D. to give
【解説】動詞 `decide` は目的語に to不定詞 をとるため、to give が正解です。
6. This is the museum ________ displays rare manuscripts from the nineteenth century.
正解: C. which
【解説】空欄の後ろに動詞 `displays` が続いているため、主格の関係代名詞 which が正解です。（先行詞が場所であっても節内に主語が欠けている場合は関係代名詞となります）
7. This is the quiet town ________ the famous novelist wrote his best-selling book.
正解: A. where
【解説】空欄の後ろに「主語＋動詞＋目的語（the famous novelist wrote his...）」の完全な文が続いているため、場所を表す関係副詞 where が入ります。
8. If David had practiced harder, he ________ the first prize in the piano competition.
正解: A. could have won
【解説】If節が `had practiced`（仮定法過去完了）となっているため、帰結節には `助動詞の過去形 + have + 過去分詞` の could have won が入ります。

1A deep fascination with youth, alienation, and the search for identity defined the literary career of J. D. Salinger (1919–2010). Raised in New York City, he began writing short stories during his college years, displaying an early talent for authentic dialogue and emotional depth. During World War II, Salinger served in the U.S. Army and experienced the horrors of combat in Europe. These traumatic events deeply affected his world view, shaping his focus on vulnerable young characters trying to navigate a confusing, insincere adult world.

10In 1951, Salinger published his masterwork, The Catcher in the Rye. The novel follows Holden Caulfield, a disillusioned sixteen-year-old boy wandering New York City after being expelled from his prep school. Holden’s honest voice and sharp criticism of social hypocrisy quickly struck a powerful chord with teenage readers across America. Despite this success—or perhaps because of it—the book sparked intense debate. Some school boards and libraries banned the novel due to its informal language and rebellious tone. Nevertheless, Salinger’s unique literary style won stunning praise from major critics, establishing the novel as a landmark achievement in modern American literature.

18Overwhelmed by sudden international fame and public scrutiny, Salinger made a surprising choice. He retreated from public life to a secluded home in New Hampshire, publishing very little after the mid-1960s. Although he avoided journalists and refused public appearances for the rest of his life, his famous novel continued to sell millions of copies worldwide. Today, The Catcher in the Rye remains a permanent fixture on high school reading lists, symbolizing the universal experience of teenage struggle and personal growth.
