ELTiS Practice Test

Grammar
Directions: In this part of the test, you will read some sentences and choose the best answer to complete each sentence.
1. Members of the Senate ________ expected to vote on the new government budget proposal tomorrow afternoon.
正解: B. are
【解説】主語は複数名詞の `Members`（修飾語句：of the Senate）であるため、複数形の現在形 be 動詞 are が入ります。
2. ________ the President sign the new bill into law before the end of this legislative session?
正解: A. Will
【解説】動詞の原形 `sign` が続いているため、未来の動作を尋ねる助動詞 Will が正解です。
3. The debate between the two presidential candidates made many voters ________ their opinions about taxation.
正解: A. reconsider
【解説】使役動詞 `make + 目的語 + 原形不定詞`（〜に…させる）の構文です。動詞の原形 reconsider（再考する）が正解です。
4. The political party decided ________ its strategy after reviewing the recent public opinion polls.
正解: C. to change
【解説】動詞 `decide` は目的語に to 不定詞 をとるため、to change が正解です。
5. Washington, D.C. is the city ________ the federal government of the United States is headquartered.
正解: B. where
【解説】先行詞が場所（the city）で、後ろに完全な文（主語＋受動態動詞）が続いているため、関係副詞の where が正解です。
6. Citizens watched the politicians ________ their passionate arguments during the national convention.
正解: A. delivering
【解説】知覚動詞 `watch + 目的語 + 現在分詞`（Oが〜しているのを見る）の構文です。動作の進行を表す delivering が入ります。
7. ________ that the election results were extremely close, election officials ordered a recount.
正解: B. Realizing
【解説】「〜と理解したので/気づいて」という理由を表す分詞構文です。文頭に現在分詞の Realizing が入ります。
8. If more citizens had voted in the local election, the outcome ________ completely different.
正解: C. might have been
【解説】If節が `had voted`（仮定法過去完了）となっているため、帰結節には `助動詞の過去形 + have + 過去分詞` の might have been が入ります。
Reading and Comprehension

1The political landscape of the United States is largely defined by its two-party system, consisting of the Democratic Party and the Republican Party. While these two parties dominate modern American politics, their origins and core ideologies are deeply rooted in different eras of American history. The Democratic Party is the older of the two, tracing its roots back to the late 1790s. It was initially formed by Thomas Jefferson and James Madison as the Democratic-Republican Party to oppose the Federalist Party, which favored a strong central government. By the 1830s, under the leadership of Andrew Jackson, the party became known simply as the Democratic Party. During this era, it championed the rights of the "common man" and argued for individual liberty and states' rights, meaning that individual states should have more power to govern themselves rather than relying on a powerful federal government.

11In contrast, the Republican Party, often referred to as the GOP (Grand Old Party), emerged much later in the 1850s. It was established primarily as an anti-slavery coalition opposing the expansion of slavery into new Western territories. Abraham Lincoln became the first Republican president in 1860, leading the country through the Civil War and securing the preservation of the Union. Over time, the political philosophies of both parties underwent significant transformations. By the mid-twentieth century, particularly following the Great Depression and the New Deal programs of President Franklin D. Roosevelt, the Democratic Party shifted toward expanding federal power to provide social welfare, protect civil rights, and regulate the economy. This major ideological shift redefined the party's modern identity as the center-left political force in America.

22Meanwhile, the Republican Party gradually evolved into the country's primary conservative force. Modern Republicans generally favor limited government intervention in the economy, lower taxes, free-market capitalism, and a strong national defense. They place a strong emphasis on individual responsibility and traditional social values. Despite ongoing debates and division within each party, the two-party framework continues to structure electoral competition in the United States. Smaller third parties, such as the Libertarian or Green parties, frequently participate in national elections, but their political influence remains constrained by an electoral system that favors broad-based major party coalitions.
